# F.40 phase 4 — plan

**Anchor:** hale-lang/hale#1212, its "Final direction" comment (2026-09-29), the phase-3 plan (branch `f40/phase3-plan`, §7 and §10), the phase-3 exit comment (2026-10-05), and the registry (`spec/registry.md`) at `main` 89147e00. Where this plan and the issue body differ, the Final direction wins; where this plan names a row, the registry's text is the contract, except where §1 says a row's text is out of date.

**What this document is.** The planning pass for phase 4, written for review before any pane starts, in the shape phase 3 was planned: where phase 3 leaves the tree, what phase 4 is and is not, the lines of work with their steps, and how the work is driven. Phase 4 is the last phase of F.40 as the Final direction scopes it. When it closes, the registry holds no legacy row, every structural rule the spec states has a registered evaluator, and what remains under #1212 is the extensions, each with a review of its own. Every size in §3 was measured on the tree at 89147e00 by a read-only survey of the rows' code; where a measurement contradicts a row's text, §1 says so.

## 1. Where phase 3 leaves the tree

- **Registry:** 45 families; 32 Canonical; 9 Migrating with 26 legacy rows (§10 lists every one); 4 Reserved (`runs_under`, `transitions`, `deployment`, `view`), which belong to the extensions and stay Reserved.
- **Lowering:** every family lowering reads is required (a located `CodegenError`, pinned by a test that removes the row) or total (the absence stated at the read and in the registry), and a guard makes a new family choose. The shadow facility has no call outside tests, guarded. Every spine is emitted from the lifecycle plan and every traced run is held to it. The entry, the placement table and the target capability matrix have one definition each.
- **Measured at the close:** `hale check dna/host` 0.75 s and 224 MB (v0.22.0: 1.71 s, 162 MB); `hale build dna/host` 17.6 s and 735 MB (30.6 s, 807 MB); editor latency on `dna/host/main.hl` 0.79 s open and 0.81 s edit; on a small file 16 ms and 10 ms (v0.22.0: 8 ms and 3 ms); the bench 25 of 29 inside their bands, with `locus_instantiation` at 1.08 and `coord_with_churn` at 1.13 outside on the slow side.
- **Exit lines phase 3 did not meet, carried here:** three derivations run more than once on a build path (`handler_rows` twice, `flows::survey` three times, `scratch_local_free_fns` twice); the two instantiation benches; the cascade model's fourth phase is not explored; the editor's final publication on `dna/host` is over the 300 ms target, and the small-file path has doubled.
- **Known-open tables** (each entry fails when its defect is fixed): the lifecycle fixtures name a bus handler's cell taking no hold on its subscriber (R52, a use-after-free that predates F.40), the owner-teardown half of a restart (C42), field replacement without a derived spine (C29), a pool child's resumed `run()` running inline (C43), structural exit inside dissolve (C25, C31); the corpus oracle's plan table names one example (C13); the wasm import backstop names `dprintf` and `fflush`.
- **What the surveys found that the rows do not say.** Five row texts are out of date, and §3 plans against the code:
  - `stdlib_surface` · `lower_fallible_call`: the function holds no stdlib literal any more; the fallible dispatch is `try_lower_fallible_stdlib_path_call`. The three dispatchers' counts are re-measured in line S.
  - `closures` · `EpochSpec::Birth`: epoch names are a closed enum the parser enforces. What is matched ad hoc is the event list of `persists_through` and `resets_on`, in `decl.rs` and `codegen.rs`.
  - `api_surface` · `generate_api`: codegen stopped running it in phase 2 (e24fbf7e3). It has one production call site, the desugar sequence, and the snapshot's configuration already carries the api entry, the roles and the environment and digests all three. What is left of the row is that `hale check` never carries an environment's roles.
  - `claims` · `validate_law_account`: admission does not re-judge. It never evaluates a law over a model. It decodes the artifact's law rows with a private copy of the law vocabulary, re-renders their forms with a private renderer, re-aggregates the stated verdicts, and refuses an artifact whose sections disagree. That refusal is a documented contract (`spec/verification.md`, the tamper tests), so the row cannot be closed as its text says without weakening it (§8, decision 2).
  - `claims` · the matrix's `constitution_identities`: it re-runs the loader and law selection, not the scope or the bus graph.
  
  And one count the phase-3 plan carried is wrong: the harness adapter `build_executable_with_options` has 470 call sites in 350 test files, not 330, and `build_executable` no longer exists.
- **Process, from the phase-3 close:** four CI failures came from a pin or a seam count a pane's local run had not covered (the cell-read inventory, the topology baseline, the placement golden, the registry's seam counts); every one is now a step of the pane's standing footer. Two regressions (the build of `dna/host` at 52.9 s; the instantiation benches at 1.6×) lived for days because performance was measured only at the exit; §5 proposes deterministic standing checks, and §8 asks for the decision.

## 2. What phase 4 is, and is not

The Final direction scopes F.40 as the compiler-convergence program: existing Hale semantics reorganized into authoritative, typed, witnessed results consumed through a shared pipeline, one semantic family at a time. Phases 0 to 3 delivered the registry and its guard, the shadow facility, the layer-3 tables, one pipeline, the lifecycle plan, the effects and placement families and the capability matrix. **Phase 4 is what the registry still says is not converged:** the 26 rows (lines S, I, A, W and T), the derivations phase 3's exit named as running more than once (line Q), and the rule guard completed (line W). It is delivered the way phase 3 was: one family at a time, behind a shadow, consumers switched one per commit, the old path deleted, the registry row closed with its code; and, where a row names a defect or the survey found one, an explicit correction with its own decision, test and review, never a correction inside a move.

It is the last phase. Its exit (§5) includes the Final direction's acceptance table, evaluated for F.40 as a whole, and the exit comment answers the acceptance question the direction put at the top: whether a contributor can make a correct change to one semantic family from its contract, producer, consumers and focused tests.

Not phase 4, by the Final direction: `@evented` (F.41), the unit dialect (#1076), a `Backend` trait beyond the capability matrix, a second emitter, footprints, storage bindings, the authority edge, the UI consumer, deployment. The four Reserved families stay Reserved. Not phase 4 by this plan's decision, each stated in §7: converting well-formedness checks into laws; converging structural laws with the user-claim judgment path; sharing the allocation summary's stdlib share across snapshots; moving the CLI's build configuration into `hale-frontend`.

The unit dialect is the next piece of work after this phase (§7), and the phase is ordered for it: line S has a lane to itself from the first day, because `Time` and `Duration` become declarations on the one stdlib table that line builds.

## 3. Lines of work

Six lines, and a seventh by decision. Each step names its job shape (§6), the model that does it, its oracle, and the registry rows it closes (the manifest in §10 is the authority). Before any line starts, one registry-only commit (**C0**, shape M) corrects the five row texts §1 names to what the code is; the rows stay open.

### Line S — the stdlib surface (layer 2), and the dispatch plan's second derivation

A stdlib function exists today in up to three places that nothing derives from one another: the registry's rows in `crates/hale-types/src/stdlib_surface.rs`, which are themselves three parallel tables (`SIGS`, 249 signatures; `SURFACES`, 424 names with their effect sets; `LOCUS_PATHS`, 67); a lowering arm in codegen; and, for some, a body in the `.hl` seeds (28 files, 9,433 lines). No row carries a lowering key. Codegen dispatches 324 distinct stdlib paths through three functions matched on `["std", ..]` literals: the statement form (`lower_stdlib_path_call`, 848 lines, 191 patterns in 156 arms), the expression form (`lower_stdlib_path_call_expr`, 1,131 lines, 271 patterns in 232 arms) and the fallible form (`try_lower_fallible_stdlib_path_call`, 456 lines, 150 patterns in 80 arms; the row names `lower_fallible_call`, which holds no literal any more). 200 of the 324 paths appear in two or all three. The statement form is a twin: 148 of its 156 arms call the expression form's helper and drop the value.

From a sample of 43 arms, about three quarters are one runtime call with routine marshalling, an eighth delegate to a Hale body, a few build IR inline, and one in twenty decides something a row should have said. Those decisions are the line's corrections, and the survey listed the population: three fallibility refusal lists (138 patterns); 21 paths codegen treats as fallible that have no signature row, so the checker cannot see that a bare call drops an error (15 are refused only by lowering, without a span, and 5 reach "not implemented"); 9 fallible rows that keep a dead bare arm; one function with two modes by position (`crypto::ecdsa_p256_sign`); one overload chosen by the receiver's lowered type, which lowers its receiver twice (`http::header`); and a fallback the expression form has and the statement form lacks. 133 of the 324 dispatched paths have no signature row at all, so the checker types their calls `Unknown` and lowering checks their arity.

The end state is one row per stdlib function, in `hale-types`, that says what the function is (signature, effect classes, fallibility, blocking) and how it lowers (`Intrinsic(id)`, a Hale body by name, or refused), and one exhaustive `match` on the intrinsic's id in codegen, with the call's position a parameter. A row without an arm does not compile. The measure is a fourth maintenance exercise beside the three the RFC named: **adding a stdlib function**, counted before and after by the phase-1 method (the places a contributor edits, and the lines read to find them).

### Line I — identities

The family's nine rows are nine identities, most of them answering a question of their own and so not second derivations of one fact, with coverages that overlap, differ without a stated reason in places, and are written down nowhere together. `hale_graph::identity` already holds the shared selection (`identity_files`: 238 files, about 12.5 MB, in eight covered crates, with the uncovered crates and their reasons) and four guards; it holds no table of the identities. Phase 3's wording for this line, "derived from the snapshot identity", fits two rows only: the replay identity's sources half and the per-file source digests. The rest are fixed when the compiler is built, belong to another artifact, or are deliberately independent of the program, and the plan restates them. None of the nine feeds `shape_hash`; two (`analysis_inputs_digest`, the per-file digests) sit inside `artifact_digest`'s range, where values may move and the section's shape may not (DNA requires schema `1.19` exactly); one (`EMBEDDED_DIRS`) is persisted in users' trees and must not move at all.

The survey confirmed both defects the replay row names and found their class is wider. `hale build` fingerprints `debug` as set and `hale run` and `hale replay` never set it, so **no recording made by a built binary replays**; the test that claims to prove a plan mismatch refuses such a recording is refused twice over before the plan is compared, and no test replays a build's recording successfully. Source paths are framed relative to the entry's parent and fall back to a bare file name, so a directory build's recording never matches its file, and two imports with one file name share an identity: swapping their contents keeps the digest. In the same class: the `[ffi]` pickup happens only in `build`; `replay` accepts `--env` and never resolves it. The stale-binary warning hashes one of codegen's 49 source files and one of the runtime's five C files, and no test exercises it. The DNA host cache's key omits the build knobs its `hale build` subprocess inherits from the environment, so a host cached under a sanitizer or `HALE_DEV` is served to a run without it.

### Line W — laws: closures, sealed access, and the rules' evaluators

Two premises of phase 3's sentence for this line ("every structural rule a registered law with an evaluator through `model_query`; rule 6 first") have moved. Rule 6 is implemented: phase 0 wrote it and phase 3 moved it onto the placement rows; it is registered with its evaluator (`lowering_laws.rs` · `pinned_features`). And the guard exists: the registry's twenty rules are `spec/semantics.md`'s numbered type-check rules, each has an evaluator, and a test fails on a rule that has none and is not Reserved. What the guard does not do is compare the registry with the spec, so a numbered rule nobody registered passes, and two other rule lists the spec states (the slot restrictions; `spec/verification.md`'s structural and design rules) are not registered at all.

The third premise needs a ruling (§8, decision 3). `model_query` today is 276 lines of helpers for user claims, and the model is built only when a program has a claim surface (GH #476's first criterion; the editor builds none), and it holds no accept or closure facts. Evaluating structural laws through it means building the model on every check, or judging laws the model cannot express. The shape that exists and works is `lowering_laws` (1,239 lines): a law is a function from family rows (the placement table, the binding rows, the ownership graph, the entry row) to diagnostics, run by the check. What it lacks is a shared witness: the four engines that render one (the call graph's, the capability matrix's, the two certificate witnesses) each have their own. This line builds on that shape: **a law is a registered function over family rows, read through the families' named queries, producing violations with one witness type.** The user-claim path stays where it is; converging the two is not part of F.40.

The size of what remains in the checker, measured: 534 diagnostic sites in `crates/hale-types/src`, 372 of them in `check.rs`. By family: expression typing 100 (a derivation, not a law, by the Final direction); forms and `@repr` layout 90; bindings and the api 43; locus declaration structure 38; bus rules 33; the placement block 24; surfaces 23; analyses whose facts are already rows 11 (rules 7, 8 and 20, starvation, birth order, nested long-running, the hot path, accept and release, instance aliasing, F.31); closures 9; sealed access 1. Phase 4 converts the families whose facts are rows in a Canonical family, and the two that close a legacy row. It does not convert well-formedness checks (a form's shape, a declaration's structure, a surface's exposure, the written placement block): each reads the declaration it judges, has one evaluator, and derives no fact a second consumer re-derives. They are registered with their evaluators and stay where they are.

The two legacy rows, as the code is:

- **`closures`.** The row names the wrong site: epoch names are a closed enum the parser enforces. What is matched ad hoc is the event list of `persists_through(...)` and `resets_on(...)`: the parser accepts any identifier, codegen copies the `persists_through` names as strings and compares them with the event being lowered, which is only ever `restart`, `restart_in_place` or `quarantine`. So a misspelled event, `persists_through(dissolve)`, and a clause on a closure with no accumulator are silent no-ops, and **`resets_on` is read by nothing**, though `spec/runtime.md` says it is honored at recovery time. The lifecycle plan cannot answer "does this locus reach this event" (one flag covers both restarts, quarantine has no obligation, and no check may build the plan), but the handler rows the check already holds can, once they also record recovery statements outside `on_failure` bodies. No program in the tree has an unreached clause.
- **`sealability`.** The survey seals every locus in a clone of the program, re-runs the whole check over a bundle that has lost its import renames and sources, and parses the message text. `check_sealed_access` yields diagnostics only, and returns early unless the receiver is sealed, so there is no row to read.

## 6. Driving the plan

The job shapes are phase 3's, unchanged: **M** mechanical (a fully specified transformation the guards check; Sonnet pane), **S** shadowed move (a derivation becomes a producer or a consumer switches to rows; the gate is zero unclassified divergences; Opus for the producer and the first consumer, Sonnet after the pattern is in the tree), **J** judgment migration (a classified correction: the decision written first, the wording and the expected change pinned, an outside review before merge), **D** design (the driver fixes the row schema, the invariants and the oracle in the brief), **V** verification (an oracle that passes, names today's defects and fails its controls), **P** performance (a measured target), **R** review (every PR, held until its outside review has landed).

The rules the phases taught, kept, and what phase 3's close added:

- **A brief is complete or the pane stops.** It names what to read first, the facts measured on the tree (from this plan's survey, with the row text's corrections), the classified divergences it may see, the change per commit with its title, the verification to run and paste, and the stop rule. A pane never decides a correction; it reports.
- **The driver owns the architecture, the decisions and the shadows.** A pane delivers thin slices. Side findings are one Deferred line in a PR body, never a new issue.
- **Corrections never ride in moves.** A classified correction is its own commit with its pinned test, its spec and book text in the same commit, and a fragment.
- **A pane verifies what it touched; CI is the gate.** The standing footer makes three things part of every change: the corpus pins a test program moves (the topology baseline, the placement golden, the law rows, the claim diagnostics), the cell-read inventory when lowering is touched, and, new in this phase, the registry guard when any `src` file is touched (a moved call changes a seam count).
- **The driver runs the combination.** The pins and the registry guard before every push; the whole `hale-codegen` crate under nextest before a combined branch goes to CI, since four of phase 3's CI failures were combinations no pane had run.
- **Every PR is a document** (What lands; Architecture, with the invariants kept; Tests; Deferred), carries its fragment in a second push when a user can observe the change, is held while its review runs, and merges on green by the driver. No attribution lines, no build in the main checkout, every worktree its own target directory.
