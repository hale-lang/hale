### Fixed
- Building a String by appending is linear in time and memory (GH #1947). An appending assignment (`s = s + …`, `s += …`, `s = f"{s}…"`) on a `let mut` local grows a buffer the local owns, and on a `String` field of `self` grows the field's block through its size classes, where each append used to copy the whole String and the frame kept every copy until it returned: a 350 KB result built in 8000 appends peaked at 4.5 GB and now peaks at 4 MB. A chain `a + b + c` is one allocation. Strings are still values: a read that may keep the local (passed to a call, bound, stored, returned) gives the buffer up, and the next append copies it (spec/stdlib.md § "Building a String").
- The hot-path allocation lint reports an append that still copies the whole String on every iteration: in a loop that also hands the local to a call (it names that read), or to a place other than a local or a field of `self`. `hale verify` fails on it.

Breaking: none
