# F.40 phase 3 — plan

**Anchor:** hale-lang/hale#1212, its "Final direction" comment (2026-09-29), the phase-2 exit comment (2026-10-01), and the registry (`spec/registry.md`) at `main` ee992190. Where this plan and the issue body differ, the Final direction wins; where this plan names a row, the registry's text is the contract.

**What this document is.** The planning pass for phase 3, written for review before any pane starts, in the shape phases 1 and 2 were planned: where phase 2 leaves the tree, what phase 3 is and is not, the lines of work with their steps, and how the work is driven. It is also written so that a driver can run it step by step from the briefs it specifies, with the model chosen per job rather than per phase (§6).

## 1. Where phase 2 leaves the tree

- One loader and one desugar sequence before the check; one snapshot per entry point (`hale_frontend::snapshot::Snapshot`) whose derived families are demanded, memoized and counted (`builds()`); the model, the artifact, the api description, the build identities, the editor's diagnostics and every editor request, and the checker's handler rows read it. Use-site identity: `Expr::Ident` is a minted `Use` site, `binding_of` resolves each use to its declaration once, and the four returned-binding walks read it.
- Registry: 44 families, 4 Canonical (`borrow_lifetime`, `model`, `seed_loading`, `working_set`), 36 Migrating with 150 legacy rows, 4 Reserved. Every closed row was one phase 2 moved with its code.
- Measured at the close: bench 29 of 29 within band against v0.22.0; the corpus AddressSanitizer oracle clean (91 fixtures); `hale check dna/host` 1.71 s → 1.92 s and 161 → 192 MB; `hale build dna/host` 30.4 s → 27.3 s; editor latency on `dna/host/main.hl` 53 ms → 1.9 s (the whole-seed parity's price: load 0.10 s, scope 0.02 s, check 0.9 s, model 2.8 s when a law demands it; a burst of edits costs one check).
- The three maintenance exercises: #1199 (handler routing) and #1210 (binding identity) moved to one producer each; #1208 (scratch-local fixpoint and teardown order) did not move at all, by plan: the lowering view and the snapshot carry no row for the entry point, the lifecycle order or allocation routing. That exercise is phase 3's measure.
- Carried from phase 2's exit and its round-1 review: the checker's two judgment migrations (unowned subscriber over the ownership graph; rules 9 and 10 over the bus graph), each measured to change diagnostics; the lowering view's own graphs (`resolved.rs` builds a scope, an ownership graph, a bus graph and handler rows over the merged program); sync inference as a pass of the sequence; SiteId joins in the model (`fn_rows`, `SupervisedRef::External`; a monomorph finds its template's rows by a linear scan; a generic supervisor's handler row is unsubstituted at instantiation); the editor's incremental check; `document_symbols` parsing its own buffer; the harness adapter `build_executable_with_options`; the api binding's generated ingress storing a per-message `Principal`.

## 2. What phase 3 is, and is not

The Final direction names what remains of F.40 after one pipeline: **lifecycle normalization** ("an explicit plan of compiler- and runtime-owned actions … a debug-build assertion checking the cascade against it, and the existing lifecycle regressions"; the first named decision: handlers run only on the queue owner's thread, so cross-thread failure delivery follows `spec/runtime.md`; #1208's second crash lives in this code), the **layer-4 fixpoints as families** (`compute_assign_moved_bindings`, `compute_elidable_methods`, `compute_nonalloc_free_fns`, `compute_scratch_local_free_fns`, `compute_stack_array_bindings`, `resolve_construction_aliases`, `resolve_qualified_bus_subjects`, the stdlib call shapes), **consolidating today's wasm restrictions into one capability matrix**, and the structural laws registered with evaluators as background work. Phase 3 is those, plus the carry-overs above, delivered the way phases 1 and 2 were: one family at a time, behind a shadow, consumers switched one per commit, the old path deleted, the registry row closed with its code.

Not phase 3, by the Final direction: `@evented` (F.41), the unit dialect, a `Backend` trait beyond the wasm matrix, a second emitter, footprints, storage bindings, the authority edge, the UI consumer. Each gets its own review once this phase is green. Not phase 3 by size: the stdlib dispatch tables (`stdlib_surface`, 612 `["std", ..]` literals in three codegen copies), the identity digests (`digests`, 9 rows), the api surface and claims rows (6), and the laws program (every structural rule a registered law with an evaluator). Those are phase 4's lines; §7 says what phase 3 leaves them.

## 3. Lines of work

Four lines run in their own worktrees. L and E share no files but the registry; P touches the checker's placement derivations and codegen's `DeploymentPlan`; C is the small-closes line, mostly mechanical, run alongside. Each step names its job shape (§6), the model that does it, its oracle, and the registry rows it closes.

## 6. Driving the plan: the right model for the right job

Phase 2 showed what each kind of step needs. The plan classifies every step by its job shape, and the shape, not the phase, picks the model and the review.

| shape | what it is | oracle | who does it | who drives it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **M** mechanical | a fully specified transformation the guards check: registry re-horizons and seam counts, signature threading, a verbatim move, a deletion once a guard proves no caller, spec and doc sync, fragments | build, registry guards, existing tests | Sonnet pane | any driver, from the brief alone |
| **S** shadowed move | a derivation becomes a family producer, or a consumer switches to the rows; the shadow over the corpus (and the binaries, for lowering) must show zero divergences; a divergence stops the step and is classified | shadow identical, suites, matrix sample, ASan where allocation moves | Opus pane for the producer and the first consumer; Sonnet for later consumers once the pattern exists in the tree | the integration owner for the producer; any driver for consumer switches |
| **J** judgment migration | the move changes a diagnostic by design: a spec decision is written first, the tests pin the wording, the fragment says what changed | wording-pinned tests; the decision in the spec; an outside review before merge | the driver writes the decision; Opus pane implements | the integration owner, with Riley's decision |
| **D** design | a table or plan that does not exist yet (the lifecycle plan, the capability matrix, the typed-body table, the dependency rows) | the table's own laws over the corpus; the oracle that reads it | the driver fixes the row schema, invariants and oracle in the brief; Opus pane implements | the integration owner |
| **V** verification | a matrix generator, a debug oracle, a GenMC model | the oracle passes over the corpus and names today's defects | Opus pane, with the existing generator or harness as the template | the integration owner fixes the axes |
| **P** performance | a measured target on the latency or timing table | the table | Opus pane with the measurement script | the integration owner sets the target |
| **R** review | every PR, within minutes of opening (Astra); a phase-level re-read after the close (an Opus reader in a pane, two or three rounds) | findings fixed on the branch with a test before merge | Astra; an Opus reader | the integration owner answers or fixes |

Rules the phases taught, kept:

- **A brief is complete or the pane stops.** The brief names what to read first (files and line ranges), the facts measured on the tree, the change per commit with its title, the verification commands to run and paste, the stop rule ("if a family cannot be switched without changing a diagnostic, stop at the previous commit and report exactly what"), and "commit verified state first" for a session that runs long. A pane never decides a judgment migration; it reports the divergence.
- **The driver owns the architecture and the shadows.** A pane delivers thin slices; the row schemas, the invariants, the decision text and the exit measurement are the driver's. Side findings are one Deferred line in the PR body, never a new issue.
- **Every PR is a document** (What lands, Architecture with the invariants kept, Tests, Deferred), carries its fragment in a second push, is held while its review runs, and merges on green by the driver. One PR in CI and one beyond.
- **No attribution lines, no build in the main checkout, every worktree its own target directory, no binary ever tracked** (the `gitignore_has_no_binary_rules` guard: build with `-o`), **no `.gitignore` rule for a built binary.**
- **Measure before and after**, under the same conditions, on a quiet machine, with the scripts kept outside `/tmp` (the reboot lesson: the scratchpad is not durable; briefs, bodies and scripts belong in the session's memory directory or the repo's `notes/` when they outlive a day).

A Sonnet driver can run line C and every M-shaped and later-consumer S-shaped step of E and P from this document and the briefs it specifies; the D, J, V and P steps and every producer stay with the integration owner on the larger model. Astra's review covers every PR either way.

## 9. Risks

- **The temporal half of layer 6** is a state-space question; L limits itself to the structural order plus the trace oracle and a GenMC model of the cascade, and leaves exactness for evented loci to F.41.
- **`codegen.rs`** (34,900 lines) is where L4, E3 and E4 land; one spine per commit, a reader pane per commit, and the trace shadow are the controls. Two lines touching it (E3's scratch-local flag, L4's spines) are sequenced, not merged in parallel.
- **The matrix's cost**: a full lifecycle matrix with ASan is nightly work; the CI sample must stay under a minute (the ownership matrix's precedent).
- **Diagnostic wording** is pinned by hundreds of tests and by DNA; every S step's shadow includes `tests/hale` and the DNA seeds, and a J step's rewording is explicit.
- **The registry as the single shared file** across four worktrees: a step's registry edit is its last commit, and the integration owner rebases daily.
