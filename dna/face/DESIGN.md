# The face: design direction

This direction follows the [visual and aesthetic guidance on issue #690](https://github.com/hale-lang/hale/issues/690#issuecomment-5718576427).
It defines the intended instrument and its visual requirements. Product scope,
implementation sequencing and acceptance evidence belong to
[#690](https://github.com/hale-lang/hale/issues/690).

## The high-fidelity laboratory

The face should feel like a precise instrument for studying and shaping a living
system. A quiet carbon field, fine vector boundaries and deliberate spatial
relationships support sustained attention. The operator should recognize the
selected object, its enclosing context and the evidence supporting each visible
claim at a glance. Density comes from useful relationships and clear hierarchy.

Recursive space is the organizing idea. Moving deeper into a locus should reveal
its contents while retaining an understandable path outward. A membrane expresses
a declared boundary; a port expresses a declared interface. Relationships carry
their own verbs and identities. Runtime ownership, organizational responsibility,
workflow dependency and authorization each receive a distinguishable visual
encoding and selectable evidence. Position context and authenticated identity
remain separately visible.

Source and authoritative services determine what the instrument can show.
Containment uses returned parent identities; Knowledge arrows use stored edge
direction and relation. Unresolved structures retain explicit uncertainty.
Compiler holes, masks and law witnesses need their corresponding typed evidence
before receiving the proposed void, shadow or fracture treatment. Layout changes
affect presentation; a future structural edit must enter the attributable command
and exact-candidate review path.

## Context, canvas, inspector, evidence

The context rail identifies the application, workspace, viewing scope and signed-in
principal. Breadcrumbs keep the enclosing structure accessible. Future acting
position controls must show the service-established authority used by an action.

The canvas is the primary spatial reading surface. Its low-intensity grid gives
orientation; membranes, ports and relationship paths carry the information.
Selection should remain stable as detail appears. Lists and outlines provide
precise lookup and an alternative route through the same objects.

The inspector explains the selected object through a readable name, exact identity,
typed properties, source text and direct relationships. Progressive disclosure
keeps routine inspection compact while preserving complete identifiers and
provenance. On narrow screens, canvas/list and inspector become deliberate stages
with a clear return path.

The evidence strip identifies the captured source, revision and freshness.
Expandable details expose the relevant catalog, projection, visibility or
dependency basis. Loading, stale data, unavailable evidence and missing objects
receive explicit states. A failed read clears data whose readability can no
longer be established.

## Three evidence strata

**Structure** is the declaration blueprint: recursively bounded loci, declared
interfaces and source-grounded relationships. Containment and definition views
must state their source and coverage. A page-bounded canvas keeps outside-page
parent references explicit. Geometric zoom and fit resize a view; semantic zoom
changes the detail shown at a recursive scope while preserving identity and
navigation.

**Ledger** is the intended active-work plane: admitted runs, attempts, barriers
and observed delivery associated with exact structural identities. The flower
and flow instruments require measured channels, pressure and causal evidence.
An unavailable plane needs a clear explanation.

**Record** is the intended durable evidence plane, with inspectable decisions and
historical context. Practices, Reviews and Knowledge must retain exact identities
and captured evidence. A map identifies which direct relationships it includes.
Record scrubbing requires authoritative historical snapshots and must distinguish
the selected historical state from present visibility and authority. Workspace
navigation, composited layers and historical inspection need distinct controls.

## Color, typography and density

Use muted carbon and slate surfaces around `#0D0D11` and `#14141A`, with restrained
depth changes and crisp rules. Off-white carries primary content. Technical cyan
and blue emphasize focus and evidenced pathways; accompanying labels establish
what each pathway means. Amber identifies unresolved or waiting conditions;
coral marks failures or witnessed violations. Every state also has text or shape.
Selection, activity and authority remain separate concepts.

Use a technical sans-serif for reading and monospace for identifiers, coordinates
and values. Prefer local font stacks, readable body sizes and tabular numerals.
Build spacing from a consistent four-pixel rhythm, with tighter property groups
and larger gaps between concerns. Square or subtly chamfered boundaries reinforce
the instrument vocabulary. Long names, Unicode and complete opaque IDs must fit
without displacing controls or obscuring relationships.

## Interaction and visual acceptance

Hover, focus and selection should respond quickly and predictably. Keyboard
navigation, visible focus, semantic headings, labeled controls and text/list
equivalents make the instrument operable without spatial pointing. Preserve
selection, target context, snapshot and paging through deep links and history.
Honor reduced motion; future parallax, morphing and causal animation must have
a stable, fully informative presentation without movement.

Visual review must demonstrate a clear context–canvas–inspector hierarchy at
desktop and narrow widths, legible contrast, intentional alignment, unclipped
focus and usable empty/error states. Review dense data and long source text,
not only an attractive small graph. Every drawn connection must be traceable
to returned evidence. Screenshots and interaction checks against identified
providers establish acceptance for that slice.
